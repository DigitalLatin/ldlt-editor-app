          <tei-div type="section" xml:id="preface-subsection-2.1" id="preface-subsection-2.1" data-origname="div">
            <tei-head xml:id="d2e388" id="d2e388" data-origname="head">2.1 Introduction</tei-head>
            <tei-p n="1" xml:id="d2e390" id="d2e390" data-origname="p">Establishing the best text of Pietro d’Abano’s commentary is no simple task,
              for it survives in several different forms. By analyzing variants in the introduction
              with which Pietro starts his commentary on the <tei-title ref="#ArPr" xml:id="d2e392" id="d2e392" data-origname="title">Problems</tei-title>,
              Gijs Coucke has demonstrated that Pietro produced a revised version of his commentary
              after the initial version was complete (<tei-ref target="Coucke2008" xml:id="d2e395" id="d2e395" data-origname="ref">2008</tei-ref>, vol. 2,
              xlii–xliv). About a quarter of the extant manuscripts derive from the initial version,
              another quarter from the revision, and the remaining half of the manuscripts descend
              from the revision made by Jean de Jandun (concerning which see <tei-ref target="Swaenepoel2009" xml:id="d2e398" id="d2e398" data-origname="ref">Swaenepoel 2009</tei-ref>). While these latter manuscripts do
              not aid in the reconstruction of the text(s) Pietro himself wrote, they provide
              important testimony as to the work’s reception in the Parisian university scene, and
              consequently I have recorded variants from five selected manuscripts in the critical
              apparatus. Coucke notes that “all manuscripts appear to have the identical explicit,
              which refers to 1310 as the year in which Peter finished his <tei-title xml:id="d2e401" id="d2e401" data-origname="title">Expositio</tei-title>”
                (<tei-ref target="Coucke2008" xml:id="d2e403" id="d2e403" data-origname="ref">2008</tei-ref>, vol. 2, xliv)—that presumably being the date
              when the initial version was completed.</tei-p>
            <tei-p n="2" xml:id="d2e407" id="d2e407" data-origname="p">Further complicating matters, some of the manuscripts change affiliations
              partway through the lengthy work. As Joan Cadden has observed, manuscript P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e409" id="d2e409" data-origname="hi">z</tei-hi>
              <tei-quote rend="inline" xml:id="d2e412" id="d2e412" data-origname="quote">contains the first half of Pietro’s version and the second half
                of Jean’s,</tei-quote> and manuscript E<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e415" id="d2e415" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi> has the converse
              arrangement (<tei-ref target="Cadden2013" xml:id="d2e418" id="d2e418" data-origname="ref">2013</tei-ref>, 228). Additionally, in his edition
              of section IV of the <tei-title xml:id="d2e421" id="d2e421" data-origname="title">Expositio</tei-title>, Coucke identifies manuscript V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e424" id="d2e424" data-origname="hi">b</tei-hi> as deriving from Pietro’s revised version, whereas by
              section XIX it hews more closely to the initial version. (Furthermore, manuscript P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e427" id="d2e427" data-origname="hi">q</tei-hi> contains Jean’s version in sections IV and XIX, but by
              section XXVII it appears to have changed to Pietro’s version [<tei-ref target="Coucke2008" xml:id="d2e430" id="d2e430" data-origname="ref">Coucke 2008</tei-ref>, vol. 2, xxvi].) As a result, I have preserved Coucke’s
              manuscript sigils, but not his labeling of families and conjectured sub-types. For
              instance, Coucke labels Pietro’s first and second versions <tei-emph rendition="#bold" xml:id="d2e433" id="d2e433" data-origname="emph">P<tei-emph rendition="#sub" xml:id="d2e435" id="d2e435" data-origname="emph">a</tei-emph></tei-emph> and <tei-emph rendition="#bold" xml:id="d2e438" id="d2e438" data-origname="emph">P<tei-emph rendition="#sub" xml:id="d2e440" id="d2e440" data-origname="emph">b</tei-emph></tei-emph>, respectively whereas I call them α and κ.             <tei-p n="1" xml:id="d2e992" id="d2e992" data-origname="p">The first version of Pietro’s commentary on section XIX of the <tei-title ref="#ArPr" xml:id="d2e994" id="d2e994" data-origname="title">Problems</tei-title>, which I label α, is found in five manuscripts: N<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e997" id="d2e997" data-origname="hi">u</tei-hi>, P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1000" id="d2e1000" data-origname="hi">y</tei-hi>, V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1003" id="d2e1003" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi>, V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1006" id="d2e1006" data-origname="hi">b</tei-hi>, and E<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1010" id="d2e1010" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi>.

            <tei-p n="1" xml:id="d2e1291" id="d2e1291" data-origname="p">The redaction undertaken by Jean de Jandun necessarily cannot help reconstruct
              the best text of Pietro’s commentary, but it is still of some interest. This is in
              part because <tei-quote rend="inline" xml:id="d2e1293" id="d2e1293" data-origname="quote">Jandun is one of the most important thinkers in the
                Latin Averroist tradition </tei-quote>(<tei-ref target="South2002" xml:id="d2e1296" id="d2e1296" data-origname="ref">South 2002</tei-ref>, 372),
              and consequently his understanding of the <tei-title ref="#ArPr" xml:id="d2e1299" id="d2e1299" data-origname="title">Problems</tei-title> and
              Pietro’s commentary merits attention. Furthermore, due to the significant circulation
              of Jean’s redaction in the environs of the University of Paris it is valuable for
              understanding the <tei-title ref="#ArPr" xml:id="d2e1302" id="d2e1302" data-origname="title">Problems</tei-title>’ reception. 
              
              As a result, the xml
              version of this edition includes readings from five selected manuscripts of Jean’s 
              redaction. In the interest of brevity, this pdf version usually presents only the best 
              reading from that recension, unless other variants are of particular interest.</tei-p>
            <tei-p n="2" xml:id="d2e1306" id="d2e1306" data-origname="p">Three broad feature of Jean’s redaction identified by Coucke in section IV also
              occur in section XIX. First, the redaction is most saliently characterized by
              omissions that <tei-quote rend="inline" xml:id="d2e1308" id="d2e1308" data-origname="quote">either cover well defined text-units (paragraphs,
                or entire (subordinate) clauses), or entail the removal of repetitive or superfluous
                speech</tei-quote> (<tei-ref target="Coucke2008" xml:id="d2e1311" id="d2e1311" data-origname="ref">2008</tei-ref>, vol. 2: xxvii). Second, as a
              whole the manuscripts of Jean’s redaction largely agree with those of Pietro’s first
              version, but on occasion they follow his second version instead, as Coucke found
              (xxx–xxxi). Third, as in section IV manuscript Vo appears to be most valuable for
              establishing the text of the redaction (xxxiii). It has almost no unique errors of any
              importance, but a brief passage in problem 9 (<tei-foreign xml:lang="la" xml:id="d2e1314" id="d2e1314" data-origname="foreign">vtrobique
              existente. Et hoc quia in cantu</tei-foreign>), which it alone omits, indicates that it
              cannot be the direct (or at least the sole) model for the rest of the redaction’s
              manuscripts.</tei-p>
            <tei-p n="3" xml:id="d2e1318" id="d2e1318" data-origname="p">Beyond these broad characteristics, however, further clarity is elusive.
              Whereas Coucke found that manuscripts P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1320" id="d2e1320" data-origname="hi">p</tei-hi> and V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1323" id="d2e1323" data-origname="hi">c</tei-hi> are “contaminated,” since in section IV they complete the
              omissions which are otherwise characteristic of Jean’s redaction (xxxi–xxxii), in
              section XIX P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1326" id="d2e1326" data-origname="hi">p</tei-hi> never does so and V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1329" id="d2e1329" data-origname="hi">c</tei-hi> only sometimes does. Coucke then goes on to note of section IV (and he
              observes that Delaurenti’s [then]-in-progress edition of section VII confirms the
              same), that: <tei-quote rend="blockquote" source="Coucke2008" xml:id="d2e1332" id="d2e1332" data-origname="quote">The other representatives of
                J [Jean’s redaction], however, are hardly differentiated, and among them the
                relations are volatile and changing. Yet, the samples allow for a division of the
                manuscripts along two main branches: (1) one of them is built around V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1334" id="d2e1334" data-origname="hi">o</tei-hi>, C<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1337" id="d2e1337" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi> and E<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1340" id="d2e1340" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi>; (2) another unites the distinct pair P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1343" id="d2e1343" data-origname="hi">q</tei-hi> P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1346" id="d2e1346" data-origname="hi">u</tei-hi> (xxxii).</tei-quote> Broad
              outlines of these relations among manuscripts are still present in section XIX: in the
              first branch, C<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1351" id="d2e1351" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi> and V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1354" id="d2e1354" data-origname="hi">o</tei-hi> are
              still closely related, but E<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1357" id="d2e1357" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi> now aligns better with
              Pietro’s first version. In the second branch, moreover, frequent agreements of P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1360" id="d2e1360" data-origname="hi">q</tei-hi> with G<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1363" id="d2e1363" data-origname="hi">u</tei-hi> and P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1366" id="d2e1366" data-origname="hi">u</tei-hi> with P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1370" id="d2e1370" data-origname="hi">p</tei-hi> also emerge. The
              remaining manuscripts, V<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1373" id="d2e1373" data-origname="hi">c</tei-hi>, P<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1376" id="d2e1376" data-origname="hi">a</tei-hi>,
              and S<tei-hi rend="superscript" xml:id="d2e1379" id="d2e1379" data-origname="hi">c</tei-hi>, do not clearly align with either branch.</tei-p>
            <tei-p n="4" xml:id="d2e1383" id="d2e1383" data-origname="p">Given that Jean’s redaction cannot help to establish Pietro’s text, I have not
